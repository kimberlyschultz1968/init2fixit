Where is every unit, right now?

One screen: what's out, what's due back today, what's overdue, what's waiting on service, and what's ready to go out again. This is the question a spreadsheet can never answer on the first try — because by the time you've scrolled to the row, somebody has already called the yard to ask.

Overdue units stop hiding

Three units a month coming back a week late is real money nobody invoiced. On a spreadsheet it goes unnoticed until the customer finally returns it.

Service stops being a black hole

A unit waiting on a part isn't available and isn't earning. That should be visible to whoever is answering the phone, not just to the shop.

The counter can answer instantly

"Do you have a 40-foot boom for Thursday?" gets answered off this screen instead of a walk through the yard and two phone calls back.

Check a unit out

What the counter fills in when something leaves the yard.

The unit moves to Out on the board, the customer is attached to it, and a timestamped entry lands in the activity log with the name of whoever did it.

Check a unit back in

What happens when it comes off the truck.

If it needs work it goes straight to In service instead of Ready, so nobody promises it to a customer on Thursday.

Every move has a name on it

Who checked it out, who took it back, and when. Not to police anybody — so that when a customer says nobody told them it was due, there's an answer.

The customer is attached to the unit

Company, contact, phone and PO all captured at the counter. Finding out who has your generator becomes one search instead of three phone calls.

Nothing gets written twice

Logged once at the counter, and it feeds the board, the overdue list, and the invoice. No re-keying into a second spreadsheet at the end of the day.
